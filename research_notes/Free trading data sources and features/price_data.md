# Free Stock Price Data: Commercial Use, Display, and Legal Workarounds (as of Nov 2026)

> Method note: Direct page fetches were blocked in this session's network sandbox (alpaca.markets, tiingo.com, alphavantage.co, tradingview.com and huggingface.co all failed DNS). Every finding below comes from web-search results. Search results quote the providers' own pages in places, but they also lean heavily on third-party comparison sites. Clauses marked "quoted via search" come from provider-owned URLs as they appeared in search snippets. They were not read in full. Treat every verdict as "verify against the live ToS before launch."

## Is there ANY truly free, commercially redistributable EOD US stock price source?

### Takeaway
No mainstream free API tier grants commercial display or redistribution rights. Every free tier checked (Alpaca, Massive/Polygon, Tiingo, Alpha Vantage, Finnhub, FMP, EODHD, Twelve Data, Marketstack, Stooq, Yahoo/yfinance) limits use to personal, internal or non-display purposes. The closest thing to a free, legally redistributable source is IEX Exchange's own HIST download (free T+1 DEEP/TOPS pcap files, with "Data provided for free by IEX" attribution). It covers IEX-venue trades only, so its prices are not consolidated "official" OHLCV. Exchange-level policy (UTP) treats EOD data as not fee-liable, and closing prices are facts that copyright does not protect. In practice, though, the restriction comes from the vendor contract you accept, not from exchange fees or copyright.

### Cited Findings

**Per-provider verdicts (free tier)**

No part of the Service or Content may be copied, reproduced, ... [to any] computer, server, web site or other medium for publication or distribution or for any commercial enterprise, without Alpaca's express prior written consent." — [Alpaca Terms (copy)](https://zesty-public-documents.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/alpaca_agreement.pdf). A secondary analysis concludes that the T&C and Customer Agreement §30 require written consent for display or commercial use, and that IEX's own permissive delayed/non-display rules "don't help" because the data arrives under Alpaca's terms — [GitHub research PR](https://github.com/giorgiofatica/fantasy-stock-app/pull/380). The Basic plan covers "for equities only the IEX exchange" — [Alpaca Market Data docs](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-market-data-api). Broker API is a separate tier "for broker partners building their own trading platforms," and OTC data needs a subscription "currently available only for broker partners" — [Alpaca Market Data FAQ](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/market-data-faq). UNCERTAIN: the end-user display terms and price for the Broker API were not found.
- **Massive (formerly Polygon.io):** NOT OK. The Individuals ToS grants a right to use the services "solely for your own personal, non-commercial, and non-business purposes." Commercial or organizational use falls under the Business ToS, and "you may not use a personal-use plan for that purpose" — [Massive Individuals ToS](https://polygon.io/individuals-terms-of-service); [Terms](https://polygon.io/terms). The Market Data Terms (Oct 9, 2024) list redistributing, displaying, or using data for business purposes as prohibited without written consent — [Polygon Market Data Terms PDF](https://massive.com/terms/market_data_terms.pdf). The free "Stocks Basic" plan gives EOD data, 5 calls/min and 2 years of history. Third parties cite a Business plan from about $2,499/mo for stocks (UNVERIFIED) — [narwhalapi review](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/massive-review); [moneyflock](https://www.moneyflock.com/contents/articles/free-stock-api-commercial-use-licensing).
- **Tiingo:** NOT OK on the free tier, but it has the cheapest published display license. The docs say Basic/Power data is "for internal and personal use only. You may not redistribute the data in any form." Redistribution "in a presentation, website, or app" requires contacting Tiingo for a license. A carve-out covers software where users supply their own API token, "provided the software is not distributing our data" — [Tiingo documentation](https://www.tiingo.com/documentation/). Product pages: "self-serve plans are licensed for internal use only" — [Tiingo IEX product](https://www.tiingo.com/products/iex); [Tiingo EOD product](https://www.tiingo.com/products/end-of-day-stock-price-data).
- **Alpha Vantage:** NOT OK. The license is "for personal, non-commercial use, unless you and Alpha Vantage have agreed otherwise in writing." A secondary source reads the terms to mean that showing data to others "directly or indirectly" counts as commercial use. Commercial access goes through premium@alphavantage.co with no published price — [Alpha Vantage ToS](https://www.alphavantage.co/terms_of_service/); [qveris free API comparison](https://qveris.ai/guides/free-financial-api-comparison); [narwhalapi](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/alpha-vantage-review). One guide mentions an exception for verified open-source or educational projects (UNVERIFIED).
- **Finnhub:** NOT OK. Per secondary sources, all self-serve plans are personal-use, redistributing "data or derived results" needs written approval, and a personal plan can't be used by a business even internally — [GitHub ZeroChart PR #109](https://github.com/blackhebrewisraeli/ZeroChart/pull/109); [moneyflock](https://www.moneyflock.com/contents/articles/free-stock-api-commercial-use-licensing). Finnhub has posted that a Performance Comparison widget is "free to use and embed to any of your website" (undated social post, not ToS) — [Finnhub on X](https://x.com/Finnhub_io).
- **Financial Modeling Prep:** NOT OK. The free Basic tier allows 250 calls/day, EOD only. External display or redistribution reportedly needs Enterprise (custom price) — [edgrapi FMP pricing](https://edgrapi.com/blog/financial-modeling-prep-api-pricing); [findmymoat](https://www.findmymoat.com/vs/eodhd-vs-financial-modeling-prep-fmp). Secondary sources only.
- **EODHD:** NOT OK. The free tier is a sandbox of about 20 calls/day, reportedly demo tickers only. Personal plans "are not a substitute for commercial display or redistribution rights," and separate B2B plans exist. A commercial internal-use plan was reported at about $399/mo (UNVERIFIED) — [findmymoat EODHD alternatives](https://www.findmymoat.com/alternatives/eodhd).
- **Twelve Data:** NOT OK on free, but it has the most transparent ladder. Reported tiers: Basic $0 (internal, non-display), Grow $79 (internal display), Venture $499 (external display), Enterprise $1,099 (external distribution). The free tier gives 8 credits/min and 800/day — [wisesheets](https://blog.wisesheets.io/best-stock-and-financial-data-apis/); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/kevin_menesesgonzlez/how-to-choose-the-best-stock-api-in-2026-1674). Secondary sources; confirm on twelvedata.com/pricing.
- **Marketstack:** NOT OK on free. The free plan lists "Non-Commercial Use," and the $9.99 plan lists "Commercial Use," but no display or redistribution terms were found — [moneyflock](https://www.moneyflock.com/contents/articles/free-stock-api-commercial-use-licensing); [ZeroChart PR #109](https://github.com/blackhebrewisraeli/ZeroChart/pull/109). UNCERTAIN: "Commercial Use" may not include display to paying users. Worth a direct written question, since this could be the cheapest option.
- **Stooq:** NOT OK. "Redistribution of data found on the website is not allowed without the consent of Stooq." Index data may be used "only for your own personal, non-commercial purposes" — [Stooq terms](https://stooq.com/terms.html).
- **Yahoo Finance / yfinance:** NOT OK. The yfinance README says it is not affiliated with Yahoo, that the Yahoo finance API "is intended for personal use only," and that users should refer to Yahoo's terms "for details on your rights to use the actual data downloaded" — [yfinance on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/yfinance/). Yahoo's terms reportedly bar automated collection without permission and commercial reuse — [fxmacrodata](https://fxmacrodata.com/articles/fxmacrodata-vs-yfinance-yahoo-finance).
- **Nasdaq Data Link:** MIXED, per dataset. Free public datasets coexist with premium ones, and licensing varies by publisher. No free, current US equity EOD price dataset with commercial rights was identified — [apilayer blog](https://blog.apilayer.com/12-best-financial-market-apis-for-real-time-data-in-2026/). (The old free WIKI prices dataset is no longer maintained. This is from background knowledge and not re-verified in this session.)
- **Barchart OnDemand free:** DISCONTINUED. "With increasing complexities around data licensing and increasing costs, Barchart is no longer able to provide this service for free." The replacement requires a commercial license — [Barchart free API page](https://www.barchart.com/ondemand/free-market-data-api).
- **Intrinio:** PAID ONLY (two-week free trial). The pricing page advertises "Commercial Use and Display Rights, Business-wide license, No exchange fees or paperwork" on its Startup tier. A real-time Nasdaq Basic source is offered under business-use licensing. Price isn't public; reviews cite about $150–$250/mo (UNVERIFIED) — [Intrinio pricing](https://intrinio.com/pricing).
- **Databento:** $125 in free signup credits, expiring after 6 months, which is a trial, not an ongoing free source — [quantvps review](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/databento-review). Databento's "Equities Basic"/"US Equities Mini" bundles proprietary feeds from several exchanges "free to license for distribution, display, and non-display applications," i.e. zero exchange license fees, but only "with any active subscription plan" — [Databento blog](https://databento.com/blog/dbeq-basic); [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/databento-launches-the-industrys-first-us-equities-bundle-with-zero-license-fees-301960067.html). Databento also sells EOD consolidated OHLCV (US Equities Summary) "free of exchange license fees" — [Databento equities](https://databento.com/equities). A Standard plan was reported at $199/mo with "no license fees, full L0 (OHLCV) history" (UNVERIFIED; check whether Databento's own vendor terms allow display to your customers) — [waylandz comparison](https://waylandz.com/quant-book-en/Data-Provider-Comparison/).

**IEX Exchange (HIST downloads)**
- IEX offers DEEP and TOPS historical data "for free download on a T+1 basis," with the last 12 months available, in pcap format — [IEX developer page](https://iextrading.com/developer/); [IEX HIST announcement](https://iextrading.com/trading/alerts/2017/014/).
- Use is governed by the IEX Historical Data Terms of Use (click-through). Anyone who distributes, sells or provides access must cite "Data provided for free by IEX" with a link. IEX retains "exclusive proprietary rights" and the data is "as is" — [IEX HIST terms](https://www.iex.io/legal/hist-data-terms). UNCERTAIN: the full text was not read, and the attribution clause implies redistribution is contemplated.
- IEX SEC filings state IEX "is not proposing to charge a fee for redistribution of IEX Data," and list delayed TOPS/DEEP as free — [SEC IEX filing 34-94630](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/iex/2022/34-94630.pdf).
- Limitation: IEX's market share is low single-digit percent (background knowledge), so OHLC built from IEX prints differs from consolidated/official closes. Volume is IEX-only. Turning pcap files into bars takes real engineering, roughly gigabytes per day of files.

**Exchange-level / CTA-UTP**
- The UTP Plan data policies say end-of-day information is not fee-liable and doesn't require a vendor agreement. The delay interval is 15 minutes — [UTP Data Policies](https://www.utpplan.com/DOC/datapolicies.pdf).
- Vendor summary: "end-of-day data isn't fee liable in the US" (exceptions such as mutual fund prices). Historical data is often not fee-liable, but access to it often is — [Quodd exchange agreements](https://www.quodd.com/exchange-agreements).
- The NYSE display agreement (2013 exhibit) bars a non-professional subscriber from furnishing market data to any other party — [NYSE Market Data Display Services Agreement](https://files.alpaca.markets/disclosures/library/NYSE+Market+Data+Display+Services+Agreement.pdf).
- Nasdaq sells options EOD history (for example $575/mo ad hoc), so "EOD" doesn't mean free from exchanges directly — [SEC Nasdaq filing](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nasdaq/2024/34-101098-ex5.pdf).

**Copyright vs contract**
- *Feist v. Rural* (1991): a compilation needs minimal creativity, and protection covers selection and arrangement, not the facts. It "will not prevent an individual from extracting factual data from the database" — [ESA Feist summary](https://www.esa.int/About_Us/Law_at_ESA/Intellectual_Property_Rights/Feist_ruling_-_USA).
- "Closing prices are in essence 'facts' and are computed without ideas, skill, or independent intellectual effort." Databases of closing prices are "rarely if ever" protected by EU database right or US compilation copyright. Some exchanges use their position to impose restrictive contract terms instead — [EDI analysis](https://www.exchange-data.com/closing-prices-and-other-stock-exchange-data-copyright-and-competition-law-issues/) (note: EDI is a data vendor with a commercial interest in this position).
- Hot-news misappropriation (*INS v. AP*) survives in a narrow class of time-sensitive uses. It is unlikely to apply to EOD/historical data, but could matter for real-time data — [Bortstein Legal Group](https://www.blegalgroup.com/market-index-licensing-a-review-of-u-s-law/); [Wikipedia INS v AP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_News_Service_v._Associated_Press).

### Inferences
- The binding constraint is contract (vendor ToS), not exchange fees or copyright. Once you accept a vendor's ToS, "facts aren't copyrightable" is no defense to breach of contract.
- One theoretical route to "free and legal" consolidated EOD: obtain closes from a source whose terms you never agreed to, or that permits redistribution. No such bulk source was found. Scraping a site like Yahoo would violate its ToS and anti-automation clauses, even though the facts are uncopyrightable.
- IEX HIST is the only free source found whose terms appear to contemplate redistribution with attribution, at the cost of non-consolidated prices and pcap parsing.

- Alpaca Broker API display rights and price: not found.
- Marketstack paid "Commercial Use" scope, and whether it includes external display: not found.

## What is the cheapest compliant option once revenue exists?

### Takeaway
The cheapest clearly published display or redistribution license is Tiingo's Base Equities Redistribution package at $250/mo for startups. It covers EOD prices, IEX-derived real-time, corporate actions and the security master. Next are Twelve Data Venture (about $499/mo, external display, secondary sources) and Databento (about $199/mo Standard, zero exchange license fees on its Mini/Basic equities bundles, though vendor display terms are unconfirmed). Intrinio's Startup tier advertises display rights but its price isn't public. Marketstack's $9.99 "Commercial Use" is cheaper but its scope is unclear.

### Cited Findings
- Tiingo Base Equities Redistribution: $250/mo startup, $500/mo enterprise. Includes EOD prices, real-time IEX derived, 24x5 real-time, corporate actions and security master — [Tiingo consolidated real-time product](https://www.tiingo.com/products/consolidated-real-time-stock-api); [Tiingo EOD product](https://www.tiingo.com/products/end-of-day-stock-price-data). The fundamentals redistribution add-on is listed as both $100 and $200/mo on different pages (conflict).
- Tiingo is the one provider a third-party analysis found with a published display license (about $250/mo for startups) — [GitHub research PR](https://github.com/giorgiofatica/fantasy-stock-app/pull/380).
- Twelve Data Venture $499 (external display), Enterprise $1,099 (distribution) — [wisesheets](https://blog.wisesheets.io/best-stock-and-financial-data-apis/) (secondary).
- Databento Standard about $199/mo, US Equities Unlimited about $4,000/mo after the June 2026 change — [waylandz](https://waylandz.com/quant-book-en/Data-Provider-Comparison/); [quantvps](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/databento-review) (secondary).

- Massive Business from about $2,499/mo (secondary) — [narwhalapi](https://narwhalapi.com/compare/massive-review).

### Inferences
- At 1,000 paying users, $250/mo is $0.25/user/mo, which is trivial. The license should be bought as soon as any third party sees prices, ideally before launch.
- Get the license scope in writing, covering display to paying subscribers, storage and caching, derived metrics, and charts. Tiingo's "Redistribution" naming suggests it covers display in a web app.

## Workarounds and widgets: what is legal at zero cost, and what do bootstrapped competitors do?

### Takeaway
1. **BYO key:** Tiingo's docs explicitly carve out apps where users supply their own token, provided your software isn't distributing the data. Alpaca's personal-use terms make a user-key-in-browser model plausible but unconfirmed.
2. **Embedded widgets:** TradingView widgets are free on external sites with mandatory, unaltered attribution. TradingView supplies the data inside its own iframe, so you don't redistribute anything. This is the standard zero-cost way to show charts. TradingView Lightweight Charts is an Apache-2.0 library with no data and an attribution/NOTICE requirement.
3. **Derived metrics:** Finnhub explicitly forbids redistributing "derived results." Most free tiers forbid any commercial or business use, even internal. Derived and non-display computation doesn't help on free tiers.
4. **DefeatBeta:** Its data is a Yahoo Finance and SEC EDGAR mirror. The dataset is labelled ODC-BY (code is Apache-2.0), but a downstream label can't grant rights the uploader never had.

### Cited Findings
- **TradingView widgets:** "The use of any TradingView products outside the TradingView website, without a proper attribution of TradingView, is not allowed. This extends to any tools (such as widgets) obtained on the TradingView website and utilized on the outside resources." Attribution must stay as originally designed. Violators may be banned and face legal action — [TradingView Policies](https://www.tradingview.com/policies/). Referral links in widget attribution are not allowed — [TradingView Partner Rules](https://www.tradingview.com/partner-rules/). Branding removal is a paid custom request — [G2 listing](https://www.g2.com/sellers/tradingview-inc). An older, undated mirror of the terms says "we do not permit commercial usage of any of our services or APIs unless separately agreed." Its provenance is unclear, and it conflicts with the widget attribution model — [idgp.org mirror](http://idgp.org/index-273.html). A GitHub analysis of what the free widget permits exists — [stockerly PR #604](https://github.com/rodacato/stockerly/pull/604).
- **Lightweight Charts:** open-source, Apache 2.0. You must add the attribution notice from the NOTICE file and a link to tradingview.com on a user-visible page — [Lightweight Charts discussion #2109](https://github.com/tradingview/lightweight-charts/discussions/2109). It contains no data, so you still need a licensed price source. The Advanced Charts/Charting Library has its own free agreement — [Free Advanced Charts Agreement](https://s3.amazonaws.com/tradingview/charting_library_license_agreement.pdf).
- **BYO key (Tiingo):** the carve-out for developers whose users supply their own API token, "provided the software is not distributing our data" — [Tiingo documentation](https://www.tiingo.com/documentation/). Real-world precedent: the WordPress plugin VSForge has users paste their own free Finnhub key — [VSForge plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/vsforge-stock-market-widgets-lite/). The Homepage dashboard tells users to read Finnhub's ToS before signing up — [gethomepage.dev](https://gethomepage.dev/widgets/services/stocks/).
- **Derived data:** Finnhub requires written approval to redistribute "data or derived results" — [ZeroChart PR #109](https://github.com/blackhebrewisraeli/ZeroChart/pull/109). Twelve Data's free tier is explicitly "internal non-display," but internal use by a business is still a business purpose. Massive's personal plan bars any business purpose — [Massive Individuals ToS](https://polygon.io/individuals-terms-of-service).
- **SEC-derived data:** SEC EDGAR is US government public data. DefeatBeta mirrors it alongside Yahoo — [defeatbeta-api PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/defeatbeta-api/). SEC filings don't contain daily market prices.
- **DefeatBeta provenance:** "An open-source alternative to Yahoo Finance's market data APIs." Data is fully hosted on the HF dataset defeatbeta/yahoo-finance-data, fetched periodically (typically daily) and uploaded — [defeatbeta-api GitHub](https://github.com/defeat-beta/defeatbeta-api); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/defeatbeta-api/). Code is Apache-2.0. The dataset's spec lists license ODC-BY — [HF spec.json](https://huggingface.co/datasets/defeatbeta/yahoo-finance-data/blob/main/spec.json). The data is described as a Yahoo Finance + SEC EDGAR mirror — [defeatbeta-api GitHub](https://github.com/defeat-beta/defeatbeta-api). UNVERIFIED: the transcript data origin wasn't confirmed in this session. Yahoo does not license transcripts for redistribution under its personal-use terms — [yfinance PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/yfinance/).

### Inferences
- **BYO key:** Legally the cleanest for Tiingo, where the carve-out is explicit. Fetch client-side from the user's browser with the user's key, and don't proxy, cache or store their data on your server. For Alpaca, each user accepts Alpaca's personal-use terms. Your app would be a tool they run on their own data, which is plausibly OK but needs Alpaca's confirmation. Storing results server-side, or sharing them across users, breaks the model. It also limits UX, because users must sign up for a key, and your product can't compute cross-user or precomputed analytics.
- **Widgets:** TradingView widgets are the most common zero-cost route for bootstrapped sites to show price charts. Data stays inside TradingView's iframe, so your site never receives or stores it. You can't use widget data in your own computations.
- **Derived, non-reconstructable metrics** (earnings-reaction %, volatility): legally safer than raw prices only under a paid license that allows derived works, or with no vendor contract at all (facts). On free tiers, the "personal/non-commercial" scope bars the commercial activity itself, whatever the output.
- **Non-display internal use:** Twelve Data's free tier is the only one framed as "internal non-display." Whether a for-profit business may use it to compute metrics shown to paying users is doubtful. Showing outputs is arguably display of derived data.
- **DefeatBeta Apache/ODC-BY:** not meaningful as a grant of rights to Yahoo-sourced prices or transcripts. The uploader can license only what it owns, and Yahoo's terms bar redistribution. EDGAR-derived parts are public domain anyway. Commercial use carries upstream (Yahoo) risk and possible takedown.
- **What bootstrapped competitors do** (inference, not directly sourced): typically (1) embed TradingView widgets for charts, (2) quietly use yfinance or free APIs in violation of ToS, accepting takedown risk, or (3) buy Tiingo or a similar $50–$250/mo license once revenue exists. The several GitHub research PRs found (fantasy-stock-app, ZeroChart, stockerly) show other small projects reaching the same conclusions — [fantasy-stock-app PR](https://github.com/giorgiofatica/fantasy-stock-app/pull/380); [ZeroChart PR](https://github.com/blackhebrewisraeli/ZeroChart/pull/109); [stockerly PR](https://github.com/rodacato/stockerly/pull/604).

**Recommended zero-cost strategy until about 1,000 users (inference):**
1. Show charts and quotes with TradingView widgets, keeping attribution intact. This displays prices at no cost and you don't store the data.
2. For your own computations (earnings-reaction %, volatility), use either (a) IEX HIST pcaps under IEX's attribution terms, accepting IEX-only prices, (b) BYO user keys fetched client-side (Tiingo carve-out), or (c) Databento's $125 free credits for a one-time historical backfill, after confirming that Databento's terms allow display of derived outputs.
3. Budget Tiingo Redistribution ($250/mo) as the first spend once revenue exists, about $0.25/user/mo at 1,000 users. Get the scope in writing.
4. Avoid yfinance, Stooq, DefeatBeta, Alpaca free, Massive free and Alpha Vantage free in production.

### Gaps
- No direct source on what share of bootstrapped competitors use which approach. That item is inference only.
- Alpaca hasn't explicitly confirmed whether a BYO-key, client-side model is allowed under its terms.
- The current TradingView widget terms page (as distinct from the general policies) wasn't read directly. The commercial-use conflict with the older mirror is unresolved.
- Databento vendor terms on display of EOD Summary data to subscribers were not confirmed.
- Whether derived metrics (non-reconstructable) are expressly allowed by Tiingo's redistribution license was not confirmed.
