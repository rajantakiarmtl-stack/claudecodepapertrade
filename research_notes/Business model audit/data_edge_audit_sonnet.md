# Data / feasibility / edge audit (Sonnet) - small-cap and biotech "real filings, not hype" risk check

Date: 2026-11-19. Lens: data usefulness, technical feasibility, edge vs AI and incumbents.
Evidence note: web search returned mostly snippets and secondary sources. Items marked [UNVERIFIED] are my judgment or background knowledge, not confirmed by a source I retrieved.

## Verdict: WEAK as specified (Viable only with a narrowed, changed product)
Confidence: about 65% in "Weak as specified"; about 25% it is viable if refocused on SEC-reporting small caps/biotech dilution + catalyst alerts for a niche; about 10% strong.

Why, in one paragraph: the pitch targets people lured by Twitter/Discord hype into OTC/pink stocks, but the data source (SEC filings) mostly does not exist for the worst of those names. For the names that do file (Nasdaq/NYSE American small caps and biotechs), the data is real and useful, but DilutionTracker, Tapeboard, BioPharmCatalyst-type calendars, OpenInsider, and general AI assistants already cover each piece. The remaining edge is "bundle + alerts + trust", which is thin, copyable in weeks, and exposed to the accuracy problem (a single wrong runway or shelf number on a stock a user then loses money on destroys the only asset, trust).

## 1. Is the signal real? (evidence ranking)

Evidence is uneven. Be careful: none of this proves a *risk-flag page* changes user outcomes; it only shows the underlying events correlate with returns.

- Equity offerings / dilution: strong, well-established negative announcement effects; small OTC issuers show significantly more negative returns than large OTC issuers (small-firm information asymmetry) [Lund/OTC-issue study snippets: https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/search/publication/2167881 ; ATM-specific study by Billett, Floros, Garfinkel: https://www.biz.uiowa.edu/faculty/jgarfinkel/pubs/ATM.pdf (I could not confirm the abnormal-return magnitude from the snippet)]. Practitioner consensus (baby-shelf rule: one-third of public float per 12 months when float < $75M) makes near-term dilution computable [https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/how/how-to-spot-dilution-sec-filings/]. Caveat: dilution is usually already visible in the price by the time a retail user sees it; the useful part is *prospective* capacity and the "cash < 12 months + effective shelf" combination.
- Insider buying: real but modest and size-dependent evidence. Lakonishok & Lee (1999) find the signal strongest in small stocks; Jeng, Metrick, Zeckhauser (2000) find no significant small/large difference and profits of only about 3% a year net of 2% costs; Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski show only "opportunistic" (non-routine) trades carry information, about 0.82%/month long-short, routine ones about zero [summaries: https://www.insidearbitrage.com/academic-research-related-to-insider-trading/ ; https://www.interactivebrokers.com/campus/traders-insight/securities/stocks/what-corporate-insider-buying-can-tell-investors-evidence-from-academic-research/]. Insider SELLS are mostly uninformative. So "insider activity" as a flag is only worth showing for open-market PURCHASES and clusters; a sell flag is mostly noise (and 10b5-1 detection is mostly a footnote parse).
- PDUFA / FDA: approvals produce positive abnormal returns, larger for small firms; but the one 2026 minute-level thesis found limited abnormal returns and mainly higher volatility (esp. after CRLs); another finds the market anticipates outcomes, more so for small caps [https://projekter.aau.dk/financial-determinants-of-market-reactions-to-fda-drug-approvals-in-the-biopharmaceutical-sector-ad666609.html ; https://theses-dissertations.princeton.edu/entities/publication/bd23f089-38e8-4418-98ad-0d470d265e28]. The "PDUFA run-up of 20-100%" claim is practitioner marketing, unverified [https://dansfera.com/how-to-trade-pdufa-dates]. I found no peer-reviewed support for a ranked "high-impact" marker. Value of a calendar is event awareness, not predictive edge; and without options IV or market cap/cash context, "high-impact" ranking is largely a guess. Binary-event risk is real (volatility up), which supports a *warning* use case.
- Cash runway -> offering likelihood: logically strong (going-concern, runway < 12 months, effective shelf => offering probable), and widely used by practitioners, but I found no clean academic number on it. [UNVERIFIED as quantified evidence.]
- Hype/social: Renault (EFMA 2017) finds abnormally high social-media message activity precedes a day-of price jump of about 6.5% then reversal of about -2.5% over five days in microcaps [https://efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2017-Athens/papers/EFMA2017_0387_fullpaper.pdf]. That supports the *problem* (hype then reversal) but it is a social-volume signal, which the product does not use (it is deliberately filings-only).

### Ranked data points: truly valuable vs filler
Valuable (build these, in this order):
1. Offering capacity + cash runway together: effective shelf size/remaining, baby-shelf cap, active ATM/424B5 sales YTD, going-concern language, quarterly burn vs cash. Combined "can they and must they raise" is the single best product feature.
2. Recent/ongoing dilution events with source links: new 424B5/S-1/F-1, PIPE or registered direct 8-Ks, reverse splits, authorized-share increases, convertible notes (toxic financing). Share-count trend from cover page.
3. Going-concern doubt, auditor change, delinquent filings (NT 10-K/10-Q), Nasdaq deficiency notices (8-K Item 3.01): cheap to extract, high signal, underserved.
4. Confirmed biotech event dates when companies state them (PDUFA, AdCom, topline timing) with source link, plus CRL/approval 8-Ks as alerts.
5. Open-market insider PURCHASES by officers, clustered (not routine, not 10b5-1).
6. SEC trading suspensions / enforcement / shell-delinquent status: small but the most on-target flag for the hype audience (see coverage section).
Filler / low value:
- Insider sales and total Form 4 volume (noise; retail misreads).
- 13G/13D lists for micro caps (mostly passive filings, little action; 13D only if activist/convertible-holder intent is parsed).
- ClinicalTrials.gov completion dates as a catalyst (see accuracy below).
- openFDA adverse events/recalls for stock risk (largely irrelevant to small-cap equity risk; mostly device/drug safety data not tied to ticker).
- A generic "high-impact" ranking without IV or float/cash context.
- Charts (TradingView widget) - a commodity everyone already has.

## 2. Technical feasibility and accuracy

Be blunt: the hard parts are exactly the parts of the pitch. XBRL ingestion of facts is easy; correct *interpretation* is not.

- Cash runway: cash and operating cash flow tags exist in XBRL, but small filers use custom extensions heavily: over 10% of small filers' tags are custom [https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/news/sec-commissioner-crenshaw-urges-companies-to-improve-xbrl-tagging/ ; CFA Institute names extensions as the biggest US data-quality problem: https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/policy/positions/data-and-technology-how-information-is-consumed]. Known error types: scaling errors, wrong sign, wrong period/fiscal year. Runway also needs: cash vs restricted cash vs short-term investments, YTD vs quarterly cash-flow cumulation (10-Q cash flow is year-to-date), subsequent-event raises (a post-quarter offering invalidates runway), milestone/royalty financings, and management guidance in the MD&A. A naive formula will be wrong often for exactly the stocks where it matters. Realistic expectation: automated runway is right within a reasonable band for perhaps 70-85% of Nasdaq small caps [UNVERIFIED estimate, my judgment] and materially wrong for the rest; it needs a "confidence/needs review" state.
- Shelf remaining capacity: the S-3 states the aggregate registered amount, but remaining capacity = registered amount minus every 424B5/ATM sale since effectiveness, across multiple prospectus supplements and sometimes multiple ATM agents; ATM sales are disclosed in 10-Q/10-K/8-K prose, not XBRL tags. Shelves also expire (3 years), can be replaced, can be universal (mixed securities, no allocation to common), and baby-shelf limits depend on public float computed on a specific date from price and non-affiliate shares. This is an NLP/LLM-extraction problem with real error rates. A manual-review queue is the only way to reach trustworthy output; DilutionTracker-class tools historically use human analysts for this [UNVERIFIED mechanism, but consistent with a small-cap tool needing precision].
- Warrant tables: warrants live in notes (and exhibits), with different strikes, exercise prices adjusted by anti-dilution/ratchets, pre-funded warrants, cashless exercise. Not reliably in XBRL. High error rate when automated; only a curated or LLM-with-review approach works.
- Reverse splits and share counts: dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding is on the cover page and usually available, but splits restate history and units/scaling errors happen. Feasible, with sanity checks.
- PDUFA dates: no official FDA list of PDUFA dates; calendars are compiled from company press releases and 8-Ks [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDUFA_date ; calendar sites like https://dansfera.com/biotech-catalysts-q2-2026]. Many small-cap sponsors disclose a target action date, but some give only "Q2" or "mid-year", extensions (3-month major-amendment) get announced late, private-sponsor or big-pharma dates are often undisclosed, and the FDA can act early or miss. Expect partial coverage and a nontrivial stale-date rate; every date must carry source link, "as of" date and an "estimated/confirmed" status.
- ClinicalTrials.gov completion dates: poor as a catalyst timer. A 582-trial breast cancer analysis found 70.4% ran late, 7.9% on time, 21.6% early, average delay about 31.7 months (single study, industry-affiliated, one disease area) [https://oncologypro.esmo.org/meeting-resources/esmo-asia-congress-2023/duration-of-breast-cancer-trials-analysis-of-predicted-versus-actual-completion-date]; reporting also found estimated dates left stale long after enrollment ended [https://www.transparimed.org/single-post/clinical-trials-fraud-fdaaa]. Treat registry dates as weak context, never a "catalyst date". Showing them as catalysts will produce visibly wrong calendars.
- Form 4: structured XML, easy and reliable. 10b5-1 flag exists as a checkbox for filings after 2023 and footnotes before; fine.
- Universe/ticker mapping: OTC tickers, ticker changes, share classes; SEC's company_tickers file lags or omits many OTC and delisted names. Moderate pain.
- Alert latency: "fast" is cheap for EDGAR polling, but classification of what a new filing means (is this 424B5 an ATM sale or a base prospectus?) requires parsing; false alerts rapidly train users to mute.

Error cost / trust: the product's whole promise is "verified, consistent, source-linked". Source links mitigate but do not eliminate the problem: a wrong "$40M shelf remaining" or "18 months runway" shown with a green traffic light on a stock that then dilutes next week is a reputational and potentially legal event (see legal note, out of my lens). Users of this segment are loud on Twitter/Reddit; one viral miss is costly. Design implication: show ranges, confidence, "unverified/auto-extracted" labels, and never a single score.

Top 5 technical/data risks:
1. Shelf-remaining and ATM-usage extraction accuracy (prose-based, multi-agent, expiries); expensive to get >95% right without human review.
2. Cash-runway errors from messy small-filer XBRL (custom tags, YTD cash flow, subsequent raises, restricted cash) producing falsely reassuring greens.
3. Warrant/convertible/ratchet tables not in structured data; dilution "fully diluted" figures would be guesses.
4. PDUFA/catalyst date coverage and staleness (no official source; extensions; vague guidance), plus ClinicalTrials.gov dates being unreliable.
5. Coverage mismatch: the audience's riskiest names mostly do not file with the SEC (below), so the product's flags go blank exactly where risk is highest.

## 3. Coverage gap (does it fail the target audience?)

I could not retrieve a hard share of OTC issuers that file with the SEC; search returned no clean statistic. What I did find: Pink has no reporting requirements or financial standards, and OTC Markets itself describes the Pink tier as including "distressed, delinquent, and dark companies" [https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/six-tips-avoiding-shell-company-fraud-0 ; https://efmaefm.org/...EFMA2017_0387_fullpaper.pdf]; OTC issuers can satisfy OTC Markets disclosure instead of SEC filing; one unsourced blog says about 11,000 Pink issuers with optional disclosure [https://pro.stockalarm.io/blog/what-are-penny-stocks, weak source]. From background knowledge [UNVERIFIED], most OTC Pink issuers file nothing with the SEC (OTC Markets own filings/"alternative reporting" or none), and the SEC's biggest trading-suspension batches have targeted delinquent/dormant shells (Operation Shell-Expel, hundreds of names in one day) [https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/sec-suspends-trading-in-61-companies-to-deter-pump-and-dump-scammers ; https://www.findknowdo.com/node/138469]. Social-media pump-and-dumps also hit small exchange-listed foreign issuers (the 2025 SDM/QMMM suspensions were Nasdaq-listed) which DO file (but via 6-K/20-F or foreign-private-issuer forms, which breaks 10-K/10-Q/8-K/Form 4 assumptions: no Form 4 for FPIs, no quarterly XBRL cash flow, different offering mechanics) [https://www.securitieslawyer101.com/2025/sec-trading-suspensions-of-qmmm-sdm-new-sec-cross-border-task-force/].

Consequence: for non-reporting OTC names the honest output is "NO SEC FILINGS - treat as highest information risk". That is a true and useful flag but it is one bit of information, not a product, and it can be delivered by free sources (OTC Markets tier labels, SEC suspension list). For the headline audience ("newer traders drawn into OTC/pink stocks"), the product largely fails as a *dilution/runway/catalysts* tool and works only as a "this ticker has no reliable disclosure" warning. The tool's real sweet spot is Nasdaq/NYSE American small caps and biotechs (and SEC-reporting OTCQB names), which is a different, more sophisticated, less-vulnerable audience that already knows DilutionTracker.

## 4. Edge vs AI assistants

- General LLMs are weak without retrieval: on FinanceBench GPT-4-Turbo got 11% closed-book and 79% even with nearly the whole filing in the prompt, with hallucinated figures reported; a vendor (Fintool) claims 98% vs 31% for GPT-4o+web search (vendor marketing, not independent) [https://the-decoder.com/language-models-are-a-poor-choice-for-answering-financial-questions-study-says/ ; https://fintool.com/press/fintool-outperforms-analysts-sec-filings]. Those benchmarks are old (2023-2024 models); I did not test current models. My judgment [UNVERIFIED]: by late 2026 frontier assistants with web search or an EDGAR MCP (edgartools, sec-edgar-mcp) answer "does XYZ have an effective S-3 and an ATM, what is the cash and quarterly burn, when is the PDUFA date" in a few minutes with source links for well-covered companies, with meaningful error on shelf-remaining, warrants, and obscure small caps. The founder's own prior report documents a Perplexity finance 1000x revenue misread [Free trading data sources and features.md citing helmterminal.dev]. So AI is *unreliable* on exactly the hard fields, but is *good enough for a user to get a first-pass answer for free*, and gets better every quarter. "Hallucinating AI vs verified" is a real differentiator today but a decaying one.
- AI-native competitors: I did not find a specific 2025-2026 AI-native small-cap dilution startup confirmed by search (search returned DilutionTracker, Tapeboard, Curved Trading guides, Gunpowder daily SEC digests). Tapeboard already offers free recent S-1/S-3/424B filing lists and a paid per-ticker dilution card with a share-count bridge [https://tapeboard.com/dilution-tracker]. Gunpowder publishes daily SEC filing digests flagging ATM usage (e.g., 89% of an ATM used by March 2026) [https://gunpowder-usa.pages.dev/blog/us-pre-market-sec-filings-roundup-2026-03-10]. Fintool and Fiscal.ai-type AI-on-filings products exist [https://fintool.com/press/fintool-outperforms-analysts-sec-filings]. Absence of a named direct clone is weak evidence of opportunity; it equally reflects my search limits.
- Copy time: the stack (edgartools MIT, per founder's report) is off-the-shelf. A competent developer plus an LLM extraction pipeline could replicate a v1 per-ticker page in roughly 1-3 months [UNVERIFIED estimate]. What is NOT quickly copyable is a high-accuracy, human-reviewed, historically accumulated structured dataset (shelf ledger, warrant tables) and a trusted brand. Incumbent DilutionTracker (paid, analyst-curated per Curved Trading review: https://curvedtrading.com/articles/en/reviews/dilutiontracker-review/) already holds that and is the thing to beat.

## 5. Existing tools: does the combined page add little?

- Dilution: DilutionTracker (paid, small-cap standard), Tapeboard (free list + paid card), Curved Trading free checklists, Gunpowder digests. Core dilution covered; the new thing would be free + more automatic + combined with runway/biotech.
- Biotech catalysts: BioPharmCatalyst, Benzinga FDA calendar [https://www.benzinga.com/fda], Dan Sfera, BioPharmWatch, many free calendars compile PDUFA dates [https://dansfera.com/ ; https://www.biopharmawatch.com/blog/fda-calendar-q4-2026-key-pdufa-dates-upcoming-approvals]. PDUFA calendar is already a commodity and mostly free; a ranked "impact" without IV is not clearly better.
- Insider: OpenInsider, SecForm4, Quiver (free/cheap).
- Brokers and general apps (Webull, Moomoo, Fintel, Stocktwits): I could not verify via search what filings/dilution data they show [no results]. From background knowledge [UNVERIFIED] they show insider and filings feeds, calendars, and short data but not shelf capacity/ATM usage; Fintel and Stocktwits add short and sentiment. So dilution/ATM capacity is the whitespace; most else is covered.
- Net: the *combined view* adds convenience and a safety-first framing, not unique information. The unique pieces are shelf/ATM/warrant/runway synthesis and a "no-disclosure" warning. Pricing: users already can get free pieces; $19/mo requires alerts to be clearly better than free Tapeboard/Gunpowder + a free chatbot.

## 6. What the defensible edge could be (if any)
Not data, not summarization, not the page. Possible edges, ranked by defensibility:
1. A human-verified (or LLM-plus-reviewed) structured ledger of dilution events and capacity for small caps, accumulated over time, with published accuracy/error-rate and change history. Slow to copy, directly the thing AI gets wrong.
2. Alert quality: low false-positive, specific, actionable filing alerts ("ATM $X remaining 62%; baby-shelf cap blocks more than $Y until DATE") delivered within minutes of EDGAR acceptance; plus alert for *absence* (stock has no disclosure).
3. Calibrated, auditable scoring (publish backtests of flags vs subsequent drawdown for the covered universe). Nobody found offers this; it would also be the best marketing and trust asset, but it requires price data (license) and it may show the flags are weaker than assumed.
4. Distribution: SEO per-ticker pages and a trusted brand. Weak moat alone.
"Execution edge" as stated is not a moat; it is a head start of weeks to months.

## 7. Changes to improve the product
1. Reposition: stop promising protection for OTC pump targets. Lead with "dilution and cash-risk monitor for SEC-reporting small-cap and biotech stocks"; handle non-filers with an explicit "no reliable disclosure" state, OTC Markets tier, SEC suspension/delinquent lists.
2. Cut scope: launch with dilution + runway + going-concern + Nasdaq deficiency + confirmed catalyst dates. Drop 13D/13G, openFDA adverse events, ClinicalTrials.gov completion dates (or show as low-confidence context only), and insider-sell flags.
3. Confidence and review: every extracted number gets source link, extraction method, confidence, and "needs review" state; human review the top few hundred most-searched small caps; publish accuracy audits.
4. Show ranges, not a single score and no green check when data is missing or low-confidence.
5. Validate before building more: manually build 50 tickers, compare against DilutionTracker and a current frontier AI+EDGAR MCP, measure error rates and time; do a landing page or concierge test for alerts before subscribing to paid infrastructure.
6. Backtest flags (needs affordable price data) to prove predictive value, or do not claim "risk".
7. Make alerts the paid product (new 424B5, ATM usage crossing thresholds, reverse-split proposal, going-concern added, PDUFA date change), with deterministic parsing for the high-precision cases first.
8. Consider B2B/API or newsletter angle (daily dilution digest) as lower-cost distribution than a consumer app.
